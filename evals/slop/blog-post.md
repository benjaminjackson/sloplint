# What I learned building a side project in public

I want to talk about something that doesn't get discussed enough.

Six months ago I shipped a small tool. It wasn't groundbreaking. It wasn't
going to change the industry. But it taught me something that I think is worth
naming.

## The lesson

Building in public isn't about the audience. It's about the accountability.
Don't call it marketing. Call it a forcing function.

The hardest part wasn't the code. It was the discipline of showing up. No
excuses, no delays, no "I'll ship it next week." Just consistent, incremental
progress toward a goal that mattered.

Here's what surprised me: the people who engaged most weren't other developers.
They were designers, writers, and founders — people who understood the shape of
the problem without knowing the syntax. That's not nothing.

## What I'd do differently

I'd start smaller. Much smaller. The first version stands as a testament to my
ambition and a monument to my poor scoping.

I'd also write more. Some observers argue that documentation is a chore, but
documentation is where you discover what you actually built. It's widely
regarded as the least glamorous part of shipping, and it's the part that
compounds.

## Where this leaves us

The project is still alive. It's not just a portfolio piece, but a genuine tool
that a handful of people rely on. And in the end, that's the entire point.

You already know whether you should start. Sit with that.
